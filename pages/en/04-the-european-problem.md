# 4. The European problem

## What the EU sees

The EU, in the Country Report Italy 2026 (European Commission, 3 June
2026) and in the Court of Auditors report (24 June 2026), has criticised
the Italian forfettario regime on four fronts:

1. **Tax gap of 59.8%** on self-employed workers (€37 billion uncollected)
2. **Cliff edge effect**: jumping from 15% to 23-43% + VAT at €85k
3. **Cost**: €3.4 billion/year for the State
4. **Growth in the number of forfettari**: 52% of VAT numbers (3.8
   million)

The hypotheses on the table: lower the threshold to €65,000, introduce
progressivity inside the forfettario, abolish it.

## What the EU does not see

The EU analysis is correct on the numbers, but **incomplete** in reading.

### The tax gap concerns all self-employed

The 59.8% is calculated on **all** Italian self-employed workers:
forfettari, ordinary self-employed, professionals, individual
entrepreneurs. Including forfettari in the aggregate without
disaggregating leads to the wrong target. The forfettario is not the
cause of the gap: it is a subset of the gap.

**Large multinational corporations** evade billions through transfer
pricing, base erosion and profit shifting — also in Italy. The EU asks
Italy to cut the forfettario at €30k and ignores the practices of its own
multinationals that erode billions.

### Italy is below the EU average

| Country | VAT exemption threshold | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Italy | €85,000 | 2024-2026 |
| France | €100,000 (micro) | 2024-2026 |
| Germany | €100,000 (Kleinunternehmer) | 2024-2026 |
| Austria | €700,000 (Kleinunternehmer) | 2024-2026 |
| Spain | simplified regime variable | 2024-2026 |
| UK | £90,000 (then removed 2024) | pre-2024 |

Italy **does not have a more generous threshold**: it has a **lower
threshold** than France, Germany and Austria. Asking it to cut it is
asymmetric.

### The number of forfettari is a vitality signal, not evasion

3.8 million forfettari out of 60 million inhabitants means **one person
in 16** chooses to experiment with the market as self-employed. In
France, the *auto-entrepreneur* has existed since 2009 and has grown
steadily.

The growth in the number of forfettari indicates that **the demand for
light autonomous work is high**. It is a signal of economic vitality,
not of fiscal pathology.

## The real European risk

> *"In the age of AI agents, removing the forfettario would be like
> killing ourselves in favour of the Europe that wants us oppressed."*
> — MVB, 2026-06-27

Europe, in 2024-2026, is negotiating the **AI Act**, the **Common
Consolidated Corporate Tax Base (CCCTB)**, the **Digital Services Act**,
the **Data Act**. In all these dossiers, the official narrative speaks of
"favouring innovation", "supporting SMEs", "strengthening European
digital sovereignty".

But **European digital sovereignty** rests on **people who live on the
ground**, know local clients, maintain the diffuse value chain. Not on 5
global platforms.

The forfettario is the **proximity accelerator** that guards the local
market. Removing it means handing the market over to those who have
already won the global race.
